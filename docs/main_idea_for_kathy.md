Overview:
- We want to build a demo to help Kathy
- Kathy is a project manager, and she manages a team of developers, designers, and security experts in effort to deploy an app that is critical for the business. She struggles, she feels isolated, she feels frustrated, she feels very tired trying to manage all of the human dependencies, chase people with their tasks, remember things that she's promised and that she's got to do, be prepared for the next meeting. She struggles with the tools, with ADO. She struggles because people don't fully follow it all the time, and it means that she doesn't have full visibility. She struggles as well because communication is very spread out. It's not centralized. It's on emails, it's on chats, it's on meetings. She often loses things within those communication channels, and she forgets the promises and forgets the deliverables, and she forgets to chase people up. This results in the project getting delayed, naturally, people maybe being slower, and the timeline slipping.

Story:
1. High-stakes start
Kathy owns delivery for a business‑critical app launch. Developers, designers, and security teams all depend on her coordination to keep things moving.
2. Work scattered across tools
Updates live everywhere: ADO, emails, chats, and meetings. No single source reflects reality, and updates are inconsistent.
3. Stuck chasing people
Kathy spends her days following up on statuses, deadlines, and approvals. Instead of leading, she becomes a human reminder system for the team.
4. Risks grow unseen
Missed commitments and blockers are buried in conversations. Dependencies surface too late, and risks form before Kathy has visibility.
5. Confidence drains
Before meetings, Kathy scrambles to reconstruct the truth. She worries about forgotten promises, missed actions, and what she might be missing.
6. Business impact hits
Follow-ups slip, accountability weakens, and delays ripple through the plan. A coordination problem becomes a delivery problem.
7. The turning point
Kathy needs more than another tool. She needs an intelligent command centre that:
• captures actions and promises automatically
• surfaces blockers and dependencies
• reminds her who to chase and when
• gives her real-time project clarity
8. Future state
With everything in one place, Kathy leads with confidence. Less chasing, fewer surprises, stronger accountability — and the launch stays on track.
